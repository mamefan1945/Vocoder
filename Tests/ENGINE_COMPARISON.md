# Classic vs LPC engine benchmark\n\nThe `VocoderDSPTests` target renders the same two checked-in reference speech recordings through both vocoder engines: `vocoder_test_ash.wav` and `vocoder_test_nova.wav`.\n\nBoth engines receive the same mono input, deterministic 110 Hz five-partial carrier, 100% wet mix, and unity engine output gain. The renders are written to `build/test-renders/` as `*_classic.wav` and `*_lpc.wav`.\n\n`Tests/evaluate_stoi.py` delay-aligns each render and reports:\n\n- **STOI:** reference-based speech-intelligibility proxy, not a general naturalness or sound-quality score.\n- **Band-envelope correlation:** correlation of 20 ms RMS contours in nine frequency bands. It checks preservation of speech-articulation energy over time, not subjective quality.\n- **Output/input RMS difference:** documents loudness mismatch; it is not a quality score.\n- **Peak, crest factor, and clipped-sample percentage:** identify excessive peaks and clipping.\n- **Estimated delay and envelope correlation:** expose timing differences and weak alignment confidence.\n\nThe script writes `engine-comparison.csv` and prints LPC-minus-Classic deltas for each speaker. Higher STOI and band-envelope correlation are generally favourable, but no single metric declares a winner. STOI on deliberately vocoded speech is only a comparative diagnostic.\n\nCI uploads the paired WAVs and CSV as `PrismaVox-Engine-Comparison-<run number>`. Listen to the paired files at matched playback loudness before choosing a winner. Do not normalize source renders before measuring RMS: loudness difference is one of the benchmark outputs.\n
